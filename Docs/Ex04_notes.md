# Exercise 4: camera parameters and post-processing in URP

Built with Claude Code.

## What was built and why
The catalogue asks for a simple gallery scene lit by lights you placed yourself, then exploration of depth of field, exposure control, lens flare, bloom and related camera effects. The scene is `Assets/Scenes/Ex04_Camera.unity`, generated by `RtcgPart2.BuildEx04`: three Nisse instances on pedestals (scaled 0.6, at 7 m, 10.5 m and 14 m from the camera), a floor and two walls, a tungsten spot as the dominant key, a cool daylight spot as window fill, and a glowing practical lamp bulb in frame that gives bloom and lens flare something bright to work on. Ambient light is flat and dim, there is no skybox, so every light is one that was placed on purpose. Each effect has an off image and an on image rendered from the same camera in `Captures/Ex04/`. The profile is `Assets/Settings/Ex04_Profile.asset`.

URP colour grading is set to High Dynamic Range and the camera has HDR enabled. Renders go through a half-float target, because an 8-bit target makes URP clamp the frame before bloom and tone mapping.

## Exposure control, stated honestly
URP has no automatic exposure (HDRP has). The lecture example of the camera adapting when a dominant light is switched off is therefore emulated. The key spot is switched off, the mean linear luminance of the frame is measured (0.1207 with the lamp on, 0.0465 with it off), and Color Adjustments post exposure is raised by 1.25 EV until the mean is back at 0.1207. This is average metering done by script, written to `emulated_auto_exposure.txt`. The compensated image keeps the cooler colour of the remaining daylight spot, which also shows that exposure fixes brightness but not colour temperature.

## Lecture terms to be able to explain
- Dynamic range and HDR: scene-referred values above 1 stay in the buffer until tone mapping maps them to the display range.
- Exposure: aperture (f-stop), shutter time and ISO set the exposure; one EV stop doubles or halves the light. Post exposure in URP is a plain multiplier by 2 to the EV.
- Auto exposure versus manual: eye and camera adapt to the average luminance; HDRP offers this, URP does not.
- White balance: correcting for the colour of the light source (tungsten is about 3200 K and looks orange under a daylight setting).
- Depth of field: set by aperture, focal length and focus distance; the circle of confusion grows with a wider aperture and with distance from the focus plane. Bokeh mode models the lens aperture shape.
- Bloom: bright pixels above a threshold are blurred and added back, imitating light scatter in the lens and eye.
- Lens flare: internal reflections in the lens elements and aperture streaks from a bright source.
- Motion blur: exposure time integrates moving objects, here from camera motion.
- Vignette: light falloff toward the frame edges.
- Radiometry versus photometry: luminance in cd per m2 and the orders of magnitude between lamp, sky and sun that make HDR necessary.
